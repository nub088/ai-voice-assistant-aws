# Objection-Handling Sheet, AI Phone Receptionist
## Field reference for sales reps

How to use this: keep it open on a second screen during calls. Each objection has the surface complaint, what the prospect is *actually* signaling, a tight response, and a re-engagement question.

**Core principle:** Most objections are not "no." They're "I'm not convinced yet." Your job is to surface the real concern, not argue with the surface one. When in doubt, ask "What would make this an easy yes for you?"

---

## CATEGORY 1: PRICE OBJECTIONS

### "It's too expensive."

**Real signal:** They haven't connected cost to revenue yet.
**Response:** "Compared to what? If I told you it captures $2,500/mo in revenue you're currently losing, would $499 still feel expensive? Let's do the math on your numbers, what's your average ticket and how many calls do you get a week?"
**Pivot:** Run the ROI worksheet live.

### "I can't afford that right now."

**Real signal:** Cash flow concern, or they're not sure it works.
**Response:** "That's why we built the 30-day pilot. You pay setup only, $299, and we run it on your after-hours calls. If it doesn't pay for itself in the first 30 days, we refund the setup."
**Pivot:** "Can you swing $299 to test this for a month with zero downside?"

### "Why is setup $399? That's a lot."

**Real signal:** They think it's auto-magic and should be plug-and-play.
**Response:** "Setup covers prompt customization to your business, your service area, your pricing rules, and a 30-minute onboarding call. It's the difference between a generic chatbot and a receptionist that sounds like it works for you. We do real work in that first week."

## CATEGORY 2: "I ALREADY HAVE SOMETHING"

### "I already have a receptionist."

**Real signal:** They think this replaces their person. It doesn't.
**Response:** "Perfect, keep them. This handles the calls your receptionist *misses*. After-hours, lunch breaks, when they're on another line, weekends. Most of our use cases run alongside a human, not instead of one."
**Pivot:** "How many calls do you think slip through to voicemail in a typical week?"

### "I use an answering service like Smith.ai / Ruby."

**Real signal:** They've already bought the category. Compare on math, not features.
**Response:** "Those are great for live transfers but they bill per minute and per call, $200–$500/mo for a few dozen calls. We're a flat rate. If you're getting more than ~40 calls a month, the math usually flips in our favor. Also: we work 24/7 at the same price, they don't."
**Pivot:** "What are you currently spending with them per month?"

### "We just send calls to voicemail and call back."

**Real signal:** They don't know voicemail is killing them.
**Response:** "Industry data: 78% of callers don't leave a voicemail and don't call back, they call the next guy on Google. If you're missing 30 calls a month and even half don't leave a message, that's 15 jobs walking to your competitor."
**Pivot:** "What if we tested that assumption for 30 days?"

### "I just use Google Voice / a free forwarding setup."

**Real signal:** Budget concern dressed as a technical preference.
**Response:** "Those route calls. They don't *answer* them. The whole point here is the customer gets a conversation, not a robot menu, they end the call with a confirmed booking, not a 'leave a message' tone."

## CATEGORY 3: TRUST & CREDIBILITY ("WHERE'S THE PROOF?")

### "Who else is using this? Show me case studies."

**Real signal:** This is THE hardest objection right now, be honest.
**Response:** "I'll be straight with you: we're early. Our founder is running it live on his own pest control business as the first deployment, and we're looking for our first external customer. That's exactly why the 30-day pilot exists and why we're aggressive on pricing, we know we need to earn trust with the early adopters. The data we capture in your 30 days *becomes* the case study."
**Pivot:** "Would you rather pay 3x the price for something with a logo wall, or test something cheap and prove it on your own numbers?"

**DO NOT:** Claim Clear Slope as an arm's-length customer. It's the founder's business.

### "How long have you been around?"

**Response:** "Less than a year. We're a founder-stage product with a real working deployment. If you need an established vendor with 100 logos, we're not it. If you want the founder picking up the phone when you have an issue, we are."

### "What happens if you go out of business?"

**Real signal:** Reasonable concern, not a real blocker.
**Response:** "Your Twilio number stays yours, we set it up in your account, not ours. Your call data lives in your Google Sheets, not our database. If we disappeared tomorrow, you'd port your number to another service and keep your data. We built it that way on purpose."

## CATEGORY 4: "AI IS CREEPY" / PRODUCT SKEPTICISM

### "My customers will hate talking to a robot."

**Real signal:** They haven't heard it. Demo it.
**Response:** "Most people don't realize they're talking to AI until we tell them. Call this number right now on speaker, judge for yourself in 60 seconds." *(Hand them the demo number.)*
**Pivot:** After demo: "What part felt off, if anything?"

### "What if it screws up and misquotes a price?"

**Response:** "It doesn't quote prices unless you give it your price list. We default to 'I'll have someone follow up with pricing for your specific situation.' You stay in control of every quote."

### "What if it can't handle a complex call?"

**Response:** "Three things happen: it captures what it can, it tags the call as 'needs follow-up,' and it can transfer to your phone or text you in real time. You don't lose the call, you just get a human handoff at the right moment."

### "Will it sound like a real person?"

**Real signal:** They need to hear it. Don't promise, demo.
**Response:** "I'd rather show you than describe it. Got 60 seconds? Call [demo number] now on speaker."

## CATEGORY 5: TECHNICAL / OPERATIONAL

### "I'm not technical, I can't set this up."

**Response:** "You don't have to. You forward your phone number, we do everything else. Total work on your end is one 30-minute call where we ask about your business, then you press one button on your phone to forward calls. That's it."

### "What about data privacy / call recording laws?"

**Real signal:** Smart prospect. Reward the question.
**Response:** "Great question. We comply with two-party consent where required, the AI announces the call is being handled by an automated assistant when needed. Recordings are optional, stored in your Google account, not ours, and we follow standard small-business data handling practices. We can walk through your state's specific requirements."

### "What if the AI is down when I need it?"

**Response:** "Built-in failover, if the AI doesn't pick up in 3 seconds, the call rolls to a backup number you set (your cell, an answering service, whatever). You're never worse off than before we existed."

### "Will it work with my CRM / scheduling software?"

**Real signal:** Asking about future-state, buying signal.
**Response:** "Today it logs to Google Sheets. CRM integrations are on the roadmap, what are you using? If it's [ServiceTitan / HouseCall Pro / Jobber], we can talk about a custom integration once you're a paying client."

### "What if my call volume spikes and I blow my plan minutes?"

**Response:** "Overage rates kick in at $0.25–$0.40/min depending on plan, predictable, not catastrophic. We also send you a heads-up when you hit 80% of your included minutes so you can upgrade before billing surprises."

## CATEGORY 6: STALLS & "MAYBE LATER"

### "Send me more info and I'll think about it."

**Real signal:** 90% chance this is a polite no. Don't accept it cleanly.
**Response:** "Happy to send the one-pager. While I have you, what specifically do you want to think about? If it's the price, the tech, or whether your customers will like it, I can probably answer that right now and save us both a follow-up."
**Pivot:** Get a specific objection or a calendar slot.

### "I need to talk to my [partner / wife / business partner]."

**Real signal:** Could be real, could be a stall.
**Response:** "Totally fair. What's the one question they're going to ask that you want to be ready for? I'll make sure you have the answer."
**Pivot:** Schedule a 3-way call. "Want me to jump on with both of you for 10 minutes? Saves you the explanation."

### "Call me back in a few months."

**Real signal:** They're not the buyer, or they don't see the urgency.
**Response:** "I will, but every month between now and then, you're losing roughly $[X] in missed-call revenue based on what you told me. What changes in a few months that doesn't apply now?"

### "We're slow right now, I'll think about it in busy season."

**Response:** "That's exactly when you need it set up, *before* the rush. Setup takes a week. If we start now, you'll be capturing every overflow call when peak hits. Trying to install this during your busy season is when most owners give up."

## CATEGORY 7: CONTRACT TERMS

### "What's the contract length? I don't want to be locked in."

**Response:** "Month to month after the pilot. No annual contract. Cancel any time with 30 days notice, you keep your Twilio number."

### "Can I cancel anytime?"

**Response:** "Yes. 30 days notice. We don't believe in locking customers in, if it's not working, we'd rather you leave clean than fight us."

### "What about price increases?"

**Response:** "We grandfather pilot pricing for 12 months. After that, any price change comes with 60 days notice and a 5% cap per year."

---

## RED FLAGS, When to Disqualify

Stop spending time on prospects who:

- Want a base salary or expect inbound leads
- Have no phone, pure online booking only (we don't help)
- Won't share basic numbers (call volume, ticket size), they're not serious
- Demand custom features before paying anything
- Use manual paper schedules and won't move to digital, integration impossible
- Have <5 inbound calls per week, math doesn't work for them
- Already used a competitor and churned for non-pricing reasons, dig deep before pitching

It's better to walk than to spend 3 weeks closing a customer who will churn in month 2.

## CLOSING LINES, Use Sparingly

When the conversation is genuinely done and you need to close or kill the deal:

- "What would have to be true for you to say yes today?"
- "Sounds like this isn't a fit right now, want me to follow up in 90 days?"
- "If we did the pilot starting Monday, what's the first thing you'd want to see in the 30-day report?"
- "Honest question: am I talking to the decision maker, or is there someone else I should loop in?"
